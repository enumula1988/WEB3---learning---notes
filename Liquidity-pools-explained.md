# Liquidity Pools Explained

## What is a Liquidity Pool?

A liquidity pool is a collection of crypto assets locked in a smart contract. It allows users to trade tokens without relying on a traditional order book.

## How Liquidity Pools Work

A typical pool contains two assets. Liquidity providers deposit both assets into the pool. Traders can then swap one asset for another using the available liquidity.

## Liquidity Providers

Liquidity providers supply assets to the pool and receive a share of trading fees in return. The amount of fees earned depends on the pool's trading activity and the provider's share of liquidity.

## Why Liquidity Matters

Higher liquidity generally allows trades to happen with less price impact. Low liquidity can cause higher slippage, especially for larger trades.

## Key Risks

Liquidity providers should understand risks such as impermanent loss, smart-contract risk, and changes in token prices.

## Simple Example

If a pool contains ETH and USDC, a trader can swap USDC for ETH through the pool. The liquidity provider earns a portion of the swap fee for providing the assets.

## Key Takeaway

Liquidity pools are an important part of decentralized finance because they provide the liquidity needed for decentralized token trading.
