# Impermanent Loss Explained

Impermanent loss is a risk faced by liquidity providers (LPs) when the price ratio of tokens in a liquidity pool changes.

## How It Happens

When you provide two tokens to an automated market maker (AMM), the pool automatically adjusts the token amounts as prices change.

If the price of one token moves significantly compared with the other, your LP position may become worth less than simply holding the original tokens.

## Simple Example

Suppose you provide ETH and USDC to a liquidity pool.

If ETH's price rises sharply, the pool will contain relatively less ETH and more USDC because traders buy ETH from the pool.

The value difference between providing liquidity and simply holding the original ETH and USDC is called impermanent loss.

## Why It Matters

Liquidity providers earn trading fees, which can help offset impermanent loss.

However, if the price changes significantly, the loss can become larger than the fees earned.

## Key Takeaway

Impermanent loss is an important risk to understand before becoming a liquidity provider.

LPs should consider both potential trading-fee earnings and the risk caused by changes in token prices.
